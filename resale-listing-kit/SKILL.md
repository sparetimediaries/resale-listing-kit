# Resale listing kit

Version 1.4. Turns a camera roll of clothes into a listing kit: one card per item with the best photos in order, every field the marketplace asks for, an honest description, a price backed by research on current listings, and a clear list of what's still missing. The seller reviews each draft, makes it their own, and posts it themselves.

**The one rule: nothing invented.** Buyers distrust AI-written listings, and with good reason: they report listings that describe things that aren't there, and the seller is the one held responsible. Every fact comes from the photos, the labels, or the seller. Anything you can't see is marked `add this` or `check label`, never guessed.

## Before you start (one questionnaire)

- Which country, and which marketplaces (e.g. Vinted, Depop)? Sizes, fees, categories and rules differ by country.
- How many items, roughly, and are the photos coming in batches?
- Are you clearing out your own wardrobe, or reselling? (Some marketplaces restrict commercial selling on personal accounts.)
- How fast do you want things to sell: quickly, or for the best price?
- Your usual size and height, if you're happy to share them (for one line in your profile, not every listing).

Then look up the current fees, buyer fees, listing rules, photo rules and parcel sizes for each marketplace in that country, and note anything recent (a new launch, changed terms, an international shipping link). Cite the source and date.

## Step 1. Take in the photos

- **Batches:** acknowledge each batch briefly and keep going until the seller says it's the last one. For large batches, build a numbered contact sheet so you can see every photo at once.
- **Group by item.** If the same item turns up in two batches, merge it, and tell the seller which ones you merged so they can split them if you're wrong. If an item is unclear, ask rather than guess.
- **Pick the cover:** the item worn or laid flat, portrait, filling the frame, against a plain wall or background, nothing else in shot. A plain background usually beats a busy outdoor shot in the grid; use the outdoor shot second. Keep the same wall and light across items so the closet looks consistent.
- **Order the rest:** a second worn angle, flat lay front, flat lay back, size and brand label, material label, details, then any flaw.
- **Skip** duplicates, creased repeats and blurry or dark shots, and say why.
- **Crop out other people,** faces if the seller prefers, and clutter (bags, mess). Cropping is fine; never generate, retouch or AI-edit the item itself.
- **Never use brand, stock or other people's photos in the listing.** A brand photo can still help you identify the exact style name for the title.

## Step 2. Fill every field, from what's visible

Fill every field the marketplace offers, including the optional ones: each is a filter buyers tick, and a blank field drops the item out of that search.

- **Title:** brand, item type, two style words, colour, size (e.g. "Assembly Label wide leg jeans, light blue, size 8"). Use the brand's own style name if you can confirm it. Brand first, because grids cut the end off. No keyword stuffing or unrelated tags.
- **Description:** short and plain. What it is, fit, condition, any flaws, measurements, and a closing "Style:" line of 4 to 6 words buyers actually search (e.g. minimal, 90s, office). Measurements stay as blanks (`waist __ cm`) unless the seller gives them. If it's new with tags and the tag shows the original price, say so.
- **Category path:** the marketplace's own path, as specific as it goes. A wrong category is the fastest way to be invisible.
- **Brand, size, condition, colour, material:** from the labels only. If the label isn't in the photos, write `check label`. Give the local size and, where the label uses another system, the conversion (e.g. EUR S, approx AU 8 to 10). Use the marketplace's own condition options and colour limit.
- **Parcel size:** the smallest the item fits; flag if it's borderline on weight.
- **Flaws:** describe them plainly, point to the photo that shows them ("shown in photo 6"), set the condition to match, and price for it. Where a flaw is an easy fix, say the seller could repair it first and list it a grade higher.

## Step 3. What's still missing, in three tiers

For each item, list only what's missing, and say which tier it's in:

- **Necessary (hold the listing until it's done):** a label photo when brand or size can't be read; a second photo when there's only one; a clean reshoot when the only photos are badly creased; the size read off the label; flat measurements for bottoms (waist, rise, inseam, length).
- **Strongly recommended (it can go up, but will sell slower):** a worn cover for items that sell on fit (jeans, blazers); flat measurements for tops, jackets and dresses (pit to pit and length); lint-rolling dark fabrics and steaming creases, then reshooting only the cover.
- **Nice to have:** back views, care labels (except where fibre changes the price, e.g. wool), side views and detail close-ups.

Buyers say the set they want is: laid flat next to a tape measure, worn or hanging in natural light, the size and brand label, the material label, and a close-up of any flaw.

End with the counts, worked out the same way every time:

- **Can go up now:** nothing necessary is missing.
- **After a quick check:** the only necessary items left are reading a label or measuring, with no new photos needed.
- **After a short photo session:** at least one necessary item needs a new photo or a reshoot.

## Step 4. Price research on current listings (every item, every marketplace)

First guesses at price usually run too high, so research every item before suggesting a price.

1. **Write the searches.** For each item and marketplace: brand plus style name first (e.g. "Assembly Label wide leg jean"); if there are fewer than about 5 results, widen to brand plus item type, then to the look without the brand (e.g. "light blue wide leg jeans"). Filter to the seller's country, the same size range and condition where the app allows.
2. **Gather the results.** Use web search where it surfaces current listings. Where it doesn't, give the seller the exact searches and filters to run in each app, and ask them to paste back the first page of results as text or screenshots.
3. **Never automate the marketplace.** No bots, scrapers or scripted browsing of the marketplace's own search, even in the seller's browser, because most marketplaces' terms ban outside tools. If the seller asks for that anyway, explain the terms risk first and let them decide.
4. **Record, per item and marketplace:** the exact search used, which step of the widening it came from (style name, brand plus type, or look only), any filters applied, how many comparable listings, the median asking price, the range, the closest match (its title or link), the date checked, and a re-check date about 2 weeks later. Note anything that skews the numbers: prices shown including a buyer fee, imports from another country, new-with-tags items, unbranded mixes.
   - **Thin sample:** if there are fewer than about 10 comparable listings, mark the price as based on a thin sample and say how many it rests on. Still suggest a price, but treat it as a rougher guide.
   - **Stale prices:** asking prices drift. Show the re-check date on the card, and re-run the searches for anything not listed by then.
5. **Suggest the asking price and a private floor** (the lowest offer worth accepting). Where buyers expect to offer, set the asking price about 25 to 30% above the floor. Price at or just under the typical asking price to sell quickly, or near the top of the range, with a reason, for the best price. Where buyers pay a protection fee on top, price a little under the other marketplace.
6. **Recommend where to list it.** Say which marketplace prices this item higher and why (designer and mid-range brands often do better on one, basics and fast fashion on another), and whether to list on both.
7. **Flag the low-value items** (where the price barely covers the time): suggest bundling them, or offering them as a cheap add-on to another order.

Say plainly what the research is: asking prices on current listings, usually from the first page of results, not sold prices, so treat them as a ceiling. Most marketplaces don't show sold prices publicly.

## Step 5. The kit

**Build it from the template, every time.** This skill ships with `assets/kit-template.html`. Copy it and replace only the data between `/*KIT*/` and `/*END*/`. Don't restyle it, rewrite its copy or rebuild the page: the template is what makes every kit look and work the same. Change the `<title>` to suit. If the template file isn't available (for example, someone was given this file on its own), follow the kit style below as closely as you can.

**Filling the data:**

- `key`: a short unique id, so ticks don't clash between kits. `kicker` and `tagline`: the playful heading lines (e.g. "a wardrobe clear-out, itemised" and "One very full wardrobe."). Leave `demo` empty.
- `markets`, `region`, `checked`, `recheck`: from Step 4.
- `before`: the "Do these before you open the app" list: necessary photos, measuring, lint-rolling and steaming, posting order.
- `play`: the playbook from Step 6, as sections of short points.
- `items`, one per item:
  - `photos`: `[file, caption]`, in posting order, cover first, with the caption starting "Cover · "; add a third value `1` for skipped photos, with the caption "Skip: reason".
  - `todo`: `[tier, text, kind]`, where tier is `n` necessary, `s` strongly recommended or `h` nice to have, and kind is `photo` (needs a new photo), `check` (label read or measuring) or empty. This drives the three counts.
  - `f`: title, desc, cat, brand, size, cond, colour, material, parcel.
  - `price`, `floor`.
  - `res`: one entry per marketplace with `n, lo, med, hi, q, step, filters, closest` (use `n: 0` and the search `q` if there were no matches).
  - `where`; `low: 1` for bundle items.
  - `story`: one or two true sentences for up to 6 "headliners" with the best photos or backstory (a repricing, a flaw handled honestly, a merged duplicate); leave it empty for the rest.
- Publish the photos alongside the page (e.g. `img/a1.jpg`) and reference them by path. Resize them to about 900px on the long side.

**What the kit contains** (the template already does all of this): a receipt-style summary (items, photos, reviewed, listed, total asking, total at floors) and the three counts; the review note at the top; the "before you open the app" list; one card per item with photos, status, price, the research for each marketplace with a thin-sample flag, where to list, the missing items by tier, every field with a Copy button, and the "Reviewed against the item" and "Listed" ticks (Reviewed is a reminder, not a gate; ticking Listed stamps the card); filters for ready, quick check, needs a photo and not listed; the playbook; and a public-copy button that hides the floors. Edits save in that browser only, so tell the seller to use one device. If a page isn't possible at all, deliver the same content as a table or document.

**Kit style** (for when the template isn't available):

- Phone-first single column, max 560px wide. First screen: a small kicker line, a huge three-line headline ("31 items. / $813 asking. / One very full wardrobe.", the last line in red), a fanned stack of up to 6 cover photos, then a receipt card.
- Colours: paper white background `#fbfaf6`, ink `#1c1916`, stamp red `#d23a22`, butter yellow `#f6e7a8` for the review note, green `#1f6b45` for ready; a matching dark theme.
- Type: Archivo 900 for headings and prices, Archivo for body, Space Mono for the receipt and the small status pills.
- The receipt has a dashed rule and a torn bottom edge. Each card has photos in a horizontal scroller with a red "cover" pill, a status pill, a big price, a dashed research box with a range bar and a red marker for the asking price, and the two ticks. Ticking Listed lands a rotated red "LISTED" rubber stamp on the card.
- **Tone:** playful in headings, notes and empty states ("Read me first (yes, you)", "The headliners", "Nothing left to list. Postie: about to be busy."). Never in the listing fields: titles, descriptions, conditions and prices stay exactly as they'd be posted.

### If the seller wants to share the kit publicly

Some sellers want to show their kit off (a post, a video, a friend). Make a separate public copy and keep the working kit private:

- Crop or leave out faces, other people, recognisable venues and home details (house numbers, mail, family photos, the view from a window). Flag any photo where you're unsure.
- Leave out the private floors (the template's public-copy button hides them on screen; for a published copy, set each `floor` to the asking price or remove the floor rows), usernames, and anything about where the seller lives.
- Keep the listing fields plain, as above.

## Step 6. The playbook (check each point against the marketplace's current rules)

- **Posting:** a few listings a night at a busy time (e.g. 7 to 9pm), seasonal pieces first, rather than everything at once. New listings get the most exposure in their first day or two. Save the rest as drafts.
- **Out of season:** heavy knits in summer and the like will move slowly; suggest holding them, or trying an international shipping link if the marketplace has one and it's that season there.
- **Offers and bundles:** turn on bundle discounts (e.g. 10% for 2, 15% for 3 or more), especially when many items are one size. Send an offer of about 10% off to people who favourite an item, if the marketplace allows it.
- **Price drops:** if an item has no favourites after about a week, drop the price around 10%; on many marketplaces this notifies people who favourited it.
- **Relisting:** only as the marketplace's current rules allow. Some restrict or temporarily ban repeated or duplicate relisting, so check before deleting and re-uploading.
- **Paid boosts:** skip at first; consider one only for a higher-value item that gets views but no offers after a week.
- **Cross-listing:** if listing on two marketplaces, mark an item sold on the other the moment it sells.
- **Profile:** add your usual size and height to your bio once, so buyers can judge fit.
- **Selling and shipping:** reply quickly, ship within a day or two, keep satchels or bags sized to the marketplace's parcel sizes, and pack neatly with a short thank-you note. Reviews matter most on a new account.

## Self-check before handing over

- No fact that isn't in a photo, a label, or the seller's words.
- Every unknown marked `add this` or `check label`.
- No generated, retouched or borrowed images in the listing; other people cropped out.
- Every price backed by research on each marketplace, labelled as asking prices, with the search used, the closest match, counts, the date checked and a re-check date; thin samples flagged.
- Nothing that automates or scrapes the marketplace.
- Missing items sorted into necessary, recommended and nice to have, with the three counts following the definitions in Step 3.
- The kit was built from the template, with only the data changed, and the review note is at the top.
- Any public copy is separate from the working kit, with people, places and private floors removed, and plain listing fields.
- Titles, descriptions and playbook tips follow each marketplace's current rules (look them up; they change).
